Case 04 · Service design · Internal tool

One workspace for three teams that used to talk in tickets.

LEKAB's support, ops, and engineering teams shared a customer but not a workspace. Every incident bounced across three tools, four Slack channels, and two spreadsheets. This board documents how service design — ecosystem mapping, blueprinting, and co-design — replaced the ticket ping-pong with a single owned artifact.

§ 01 · Overview

Designing for the workflow between teams — not the teams.

Support could see the customer's problem. Ops knew the platform's state. Engineering held the fix. None of them saw each other. The product wasn't a ticketing tool — it was a shared workspace where a case could move without losing context.

Board summary
Context

18 people across three teams handling ~120 cases per week. Customer impact of a mis-handoff was measured in trust, not just SLA.

My role

Lead designer + service design facilitator. Owned discovery workshops, role personas, service blueprint, prototype, and rollout.

Approach

Ecosystem map → role personas → service safari → service blueprint → co-design workshops → prototype → dogfood → ship.

§ 09 · Compliance by design

Internal tools handle real customer data.

The support platform sees customer PII, incident details, and platform state — sometimes all in the same thread. Compliance had to be a design surface, not a legal review at the end.

Compliance decisions on the board
✓ WCAG 2.2 AA · Support Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 across all three role views. Full keyboard nav. Every case action reachable without a mouse. Screen-reader tested — the queue announces state changes as live regions.
✓ GDPR · Role-based visibility Support sees customer language + case status. Ops sees platform state. Engineering sees signatures + rollback. Nobody sees data they don't need. Access changes logged to the case thread.
✓ Audit trail · Immutable Every case thread is append-only. Edits leave a visible ghost of the previous value. Postmortems are auto-composed from the same history — no separate doc to fabricate later.
✓ Right to erasure · Article 17 When a customer requests erasure, cases are anonymized (customer identifier → hash), but incident metadata is retained for postmortems + regulatory reporting. Documented in the product's data map.
✏️   compliance decisions live in the artifact itself —
      not in a separate policy nobody reads
§ 09 · Reflection

Internal tools are still services.

What service design brought

The blueprint made invisible work visible. Nobody had to argue about whether handoffs were painful — the pain row of the blueprint showed every one of them was a manual retelling.

The co-design workshops turned three teams that had been at odds into three teams that owned the same design. Adoption was almost frictionless because the users had built it with me.

What I'd change next time

Blueprint before wireframes, every time. Cheaper alignment, and it kills feature debates that shouldn't exist.

Next time I'd run a lightweight service safari — following one case across the three tools for a full day — before the workshops. My own map would have been sharper going in.
