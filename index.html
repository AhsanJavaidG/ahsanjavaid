Compliance by design

Accessibility, GDPR, and consent — decisions I make in Figma, not in review.

Compliance rarely gets a portfolio section. It should. Every product I ship goes through the same three checks — can everyone use it, have we asked for the minimum data, and would the customer be surprised by what happens next. These aren't legal boxes; they're design decisions.

WCAG 2.2 AA Accessibility

Contrast tested at design time. All flows work keyboard-only. Focus indicators visible on every interactive element. aria-* labels reviewed with screen readers. This portfolio ships with skip links, focus rings, and honors prefers-reduced-motion.

GDPR by design Data minimization

Ask for the minimum. Explain each field. Move enrichment to a moment of trust. Progressive disclosure isn't just a UX pattern — it's Article 5(1)(c) in visual form. LEKAB's signup removed 60% of the upfront data ask without losing a byte of downstream value.

Consent Meaningful consent

No dark patterns. No pre-checked boxes. STOP replies honored globally, not per campaign. Analytics on the CRO project ran without PII in Hotjar and with cohort-only reporting so individuals were never re-identifiable — even for the design team.

Trust surface Predictable behavior

Recording notice at the top of every call. Chat history off by default. Copy/share blocked when meeting mode requires it. On internal tools, role-based visibility so agents never see data they don't need. Compliance shows up as calm UI, not modal walls.
