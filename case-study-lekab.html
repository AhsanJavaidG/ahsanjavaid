Overview

Turning a security-heavy signup into a guided, progressive flow.

LEKAB is a communication and workflow automation platform used across healthcare, finance, logistics, public sector, retail, and manufacturing — industries where the wrong phone number or missing role can break a workflow. The onboarding experience had to stay compliant, but stop punishing new users for it.

Context

Enterprise users were being asked for personal data, work data, role, department, and country context in a single upfront form. Many abandoned. Others falsified fields to get through.

My Role

End-to-end ownership: discovery, journey mapping, IA, prototyping in Figma, validation with real users, and dev-ready handoff with tokens.

Impact

Signup completion rose materially, admin escalations dropped ~30%, and data quality improved enough that downstream billing errors fell alongside it.

The problem

Too much, too early, too unclear.

“Users abandoned the flow when asked for too much upfront — and the data they did submit was often wrong.”

~40%

Drop-off before the first field was completed on legacy signup.

1 in 3

Records contained falsified or blank secondary contact fields.

Manual

Every new account still required an admin to issue an ID and access code.

High

Support tickets tied to "can't finish signup" made up a visible share of weekly load.

Research & insights

Listening before we redesigned anything.

I ran user interviews with enterprise admins and end-users, mapped the current journey with the support team, and pulled from established form-conversion research to pressure-test the direction. Three insights shaped the whole redesign.

MK

Maria K.

Operations Lead · Nordic bank · 42
Goals

Onboard her team to a messaging platform without opening a ticket for every seat.

Frustrations

Being asked for private numbers before she trusts the tool. Waiting on IT for access codes.

Behavior

Skips optional fields. Bails if she has to leave the page to find a code.

"I'll give you a work email. Ask me for my personal mobile before I've even seen the product, and I'm gone."
01

Commitment scales with progress.

Foot-in-the-door: users who complete a light first step will complete a heavier second step. Ask for less upfront and you get more later.

02

Explain the "why", not just the "what".

When each field explained why it was needed (security, personalization, routing), users stopped falsifying and started completing.

03

Security should feel invisible, not blocking.

reCAPTCHA + verified email did more for trust than a wall of legal copy — and unblocked admins from the loop entirely.

Design system

A quiet, tokenized system built to survive engineering.

Every color, every step of the type ramp, and every component was defined in Figma variables so that engineers implemented what design decided — not their interpretation of a screenshot.

Outcome

The numbers moved. The tickets dropped. Trust went up.

+0% Usability improvement Measured against the legacy signup path, cohort over cohort.
−0% Support load Fewer "can't finish signup" tickets after admin was removed from the loop.
+0% Data accuracy Progressive disclosure reduced falsified secondary contacts.
−0% Visual bugs Tokenized design system killed most implementation drift at the source.
Compliance by design

Accessibility, GDPR, and consent — built into the flow, not stapled to it.

Progressive signup wasn't just a UX pattern here — it was a compliance strategy. Less data upfront meant less risk, less liability, and less need for the customer to trust us before we'd earned it. Every step below shipped in the design spec, not the legal review.

WCAG 2.2 AA Accessibility

Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 on all body text, 3:1 on large text. Full keyboard navigation with visible focus rings. Screen-reader tested with NVDA + VoiceOver. Error messages describe the fix, not just the fault.

GDPR Art. 5(1)(c) Data minimization

Phase 1 asks for four fields, not eleven. Phase 2 enriches after value is delivered. Personal data collected drops by ~60% at the moment of first commitment.

Consent Explain the why

Every field carries inline helper text explaining why we ask. No pre-checked marketing boxes. Withdrawal of consent lives one tap deep in account settings — not buried in policy.

Right to erasure One-tap forget

Account deletion is a single confirmed action, no email chain required. Downstream systems receive a signed erasure event within 30 days per Article 17.

Reflection

What I'd carry into the next enterprise product.

What worked

Treating signup as a two-act experience — commitment before completeness — held up in usability testing and in the live cohort. The token-first design system paid for itself the first time a component changed and no one had to hunt for a hex code.

The biggest surprise: explaining why a field was needed moved the completion needle almost as much as removing the field itself.

What I'd do next

Introduce a lightweight sandbox preview before phase 2 — let users experience one real message send before we ask them for their role and department. That collapses the value-to-trust loop even further.

And I'd invest earlier in a shared vocabulary with engineering. Aligned language on states, empty states, and error states probably saved more sprint time than any single component did.
